# Reference O: Physical Production and Prototyping

## O §1 — Why Production Knowledge Is a Design Tool

Physical card game designers who do not understand production constraints make design decisions that are expensive, impossible to manufacture, or legally non-compliant. Production knowledge is not a post-design concern; it shapes design from day one.

Production knowledge also directly affects playtesting speed: the faster you can make a testable prototype, the faster you can iterate.

## O §2 — Prototype Construction

**Card proxies for early testing:**
The fastest prototype is index cards or cut paper with handwritten text. Use this for Phase 1 rules testing (see M §2). Do not invest in printed proxies until the rules structure is validated.

**Printed proxy methods:**
- Word processor template cards, printed on card stock and cut — adequate for Phases 2 and 3
- Nandecko or similar card layout software — produces professional-looking proxies at low cost
- Print-on-demand services (MakePlayingCards, PrinterStudio) — produces near-final quality for late playtesting
- Sleeve proxies — printed card inserted behind a sleeved spare card; fastest iteration method for existing card games being modified

**Prototype iteration rule:** Never produce a full professional print run until at least 50 games of Phase 4 testing (full card pool) are complete. The cost of a professional print run wasted on a game that needed more testing is always higher than the cost of another prototype iteration.

## O §3 — Card Stock and Physical Specifications

**Standard card sizes:**
- Poker size (63.5mm × 88.9mm) — most common; Magic, Pokémon, most TCGs
- Tarot size (70mm × 120mm) — used for games wanting more image space; less sleeve compatibility
- Bridge size (57mm × 89mm) — narrower; some card games, more comfortable for large hands
- Mini (44mm × 63mm) — for resource cards or tokens that need to be distinguishable from main deck cards

**Card stock weight:**
- 300–350gsm with linen texture — standard TCG feel; good durability; recommended for published games
- 280gsm glossy — cheaper; used in lighter games; wears faster
- 400gsm+ — casino quality; extreme durability; significantly higher cost

**Finish options:**
- Matte — prevents glare; cleaner art presentation; hides slight finger wear better
- Gloss — vibrant colours; easier to mark from handling; common in budget products
- Linen texture — handles shuffling better; most common in premium TCGs

**Foil treatments:**
- Full foil (holofoil) — entire card surface; high cost; used for highest rarity tier only
- Spot foil — selected areas of art or text; more design control; moderate cost
- Rainbow foil — full art foil with shifting rainbow pattern; premium collector version

## O §4 — Colour and Accessibility in Card Design

**Colour blindness considerations:**
Approximately 8% of males and 0.5% of females have some form of colour blindness. The most common type (red-green) affects how faction colour coding is perceived.

**Design rules:**
- Never rely on colour alone as the only faction differentiator. Add shape, symbol, or pattern as a secondary indicator.
- Test faction colour palette with a colour blindness simulator before finalising (Coblis, Coolors accessibility tools)
- If using red and green as faction colours in the same game, add a distinctive secondary visual marker (border shape, symbol in corner)
- Rarity indicators should use symbol shape + colour, not colour alone

**Font legibility:**
- Minimum 8pt font for body text on standard poker-size cards
- Keywords should be bolded regardless of font size
- Script or decorative fonts should be used only for titles, never for rules text
- White text on dark backgrounds requires extra weight (bold or slightly larger) for readability

## O §5 — Manufacturing Lead Times

For designers moving toward publication:

**Print-on-demand (POD):** 1–2 weeks turnaround. Per-unit cost is high. Good for small runs, artist proof copies, convention prototypes. No minimum order. No inventory required.

**Short run offset printing:** 4–8 weeks. Minimum typically 100–500 copies. Per-unit cost drops significantly from POD. Good for Kickstarter fulfillment, limited releases, event-specific products.

**Full commercial run:** 12–20 weeks from final files. Minimum typically 1,000–5,000 copies. Per-unit cost at commercial level. Required for retail distribution.

**Critical lead time rule:** Design must be completely frozen (no further changes to any card) at least 4 weeks before any print deadline. Card changes after the print deadline require reprinting, which costs time and money. This reinforces the M §2 Phase 4 rule: do not send to print until full pool testing is done.

## O §6 — Box Design and Component Packaging

**Packaging requirements:**
- Tuck box — standard card game packaging; cheap; adequate for products with cards only
- Two-piece box (lid + tray) — more durable; fits expansion components; required if tokens, dice, or boards are included
- Tin — premium collector packaging; significantly higher cost; good for gift editions or anniversary products

**What must fit in the box:**
Design the box dimensions after knowing exactly what will be in the game. Account for:
- Full deck of cards (sleeved) plus space for expansion inserts
- Any tokens, dice, or play aids
- Quick-reference guide
- Rulebook

**Insert design:** A good insert organises components so they're easy to set up and easy to store. Poor inserts lead to component damage and negative unboxing experience. For a TCG, the box must accommodate sleeved cards — sleeved cards are significantly larger than unsleeved.

## O §7 — Crowdfunding and Self-Publishing

**Kickstarter considerations:**
- Manufacturing cost per unit typically needs to be no more than 25–30% of the retail price for sustainable margins
- Shipping cost must be calculated per-backer, not globally — international shipping can equal or exceed manufacturing cost
- Stretch goals that add components or cards after the campaign's core design is locked create late design changes, which delay production
- Fulfillment logistics (packing, shipping, returns) typically cost 15–20% of campaign revenue

**Self-publishing vs publisher:**
- Self-publishing: higher per-unit revenue, full creative control, all business risk, all operational burden
- Publisher: lower per-unit revenue, reduced creative control, shared risk, operational support, distribution access

**Design implication:** If planning a Kickstarter, the game must be completely designed and extensively playtested before the campaign launches. Kickstarter is a sales and fulfillment mechanism, not a design and development platform.

## O §8 — Production-Linked Design Rules

These design decisions have direct production cost implications:

| Design decision | Production impact |
|---|---|
| More than 3 faction colours | Increases print colour registration complexity |
| Full-bleed art (art to card edge) | Requires tighter print registration; increases waste at cutting |
| Custom die-cut shapes | Significantly more expensive than standard rectangle |
| Embossing or debossing | Premium cost; requires separate tooling |
| Multiple back face designs | Requires separate print runs per back; minimum order applies to each |
| Foil on commons | Prohibitively expensive; foil is reserved for rare+ cards for cost reasons |
| UV spot varnish | Moderate premium; adds visual interest to specific elements |
