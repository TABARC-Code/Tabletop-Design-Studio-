## Purpose

Use this subskill when the user needs help understanding **how “German games” became “eurogames”**, especially through export, hobbyist adoption, relabelling, and changes in audience.

The source stages this movement explicitly in its chapter sequence: after Germany comes *From German Games to Eurogames*. That structure implies transformation, translation, and adoption rather than a static label.

## Core argument Claude should preserve

A “German game” is partly a matter of **origin and market context**.
A “eurogame” is a later, broader **genre label and hobbyist understanding**.

The transition involves:

- games moving beyond their original national market
- hobby communities treating them as a coherent family
- publishers and players emphasising shared design tendencies
- a genre identity forming through circulation and discussion

## Instruction set

### When the user asks about the difference between “German games” and “eurogames”

Use this distinction:

- **German games**: a geographically and culturally rooted descriptor
- **eurogames**: a genre descriptor used internationally for a cluster of design traits

### When the user asks how hobbyists adopted the form

Point out that eurogames spread not simply because publishers announced a genre, but because players, reviewers, clubs, and online communities recognised recurring pleasures and communicated them.

The source text emphasises that eurogames became popular among hobby gamers not through a heavy-handed single commercial push, but through circulation inside hobby culture. The later hobby-gamer chapter builds directly on that observation.

### When the user wants to position a new design

Ask:

- Is the game merely European in theme or origin?
- Or does it adopt eurogame-like priorities in pacing, interaction, and accessibility?

Do not award the label just because the game has wooden bits or a muted palette.

## Signs of the crossing

A design is moving from “German-style” context to “eurogame” genre recognition when it is discussed in terms of:

- short teachable rules
- broad hobby recommendation
- repeatable strategic depth
- lower-conflict structure
- indirect interaction
- elegant turn economy
- accessible but meaningful play

## Common analytic tasks

This subskill is good for:

- genre genealogy
- writing explanatory essays about the emergence of eurogames
- analysing import/adoption histories
- helping users avoid lazy labelling

## Answer style

When appropriate, emphasise that genre names are community tools, not laws of nature. They stabilise after repeated use. Treat “eurogame” as an emergent shared category rather than a perfect box.
