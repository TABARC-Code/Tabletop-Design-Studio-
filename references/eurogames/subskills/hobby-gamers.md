## Purpose

Use this subskill when the user needs help understanding **who hobby gamers are**, how they behave, what they value, and why hobby uptake matters when designing or analysing eurogames.

The source chapter *Hobby Gamers* treats dedicated players as more than just people who happen to play a board game now and then. It frames them as a visible enthusiast culture with acquisition habits, discourse, communal sites, and a degree of evangelism for the hobby.

## Core user model

When discussing hobby gamers, assume the following tendencies may be present:

- they seek out information and recommendations
- they replay, compare, rate, and discuss games
- they accumulate collections
- they teach games to others
- they often care about design lineage and genre distinctions
- they may act as ambassadors, gatekeepers, or enthusiasts depending on context

The source text explicitly notes enthusiasm as visible through participation, accumulation, and evangelism.

## Design implications

### If the user is designing for hobby gamers

Lean into:
- replayability
- decision interest after repeated plays
- emergent table stories
- concise but nontrivial rules
- enough novelty to invite discussion

Be prepared for:
- scrutiny of balance
- comparison to known titles
- sensitivity to downtime and stale openings
- greater tolerance for learning effort than a pure family audience

### If the user is designing for mixed tables

Remember hobby gamers often teach and host. Therefore:
- make setup and explanation robust
- let experienced players enjoy subtlety without crushing newcomers
- provide player aids or intuitive iconography
- avoid designs that require encyclopedic rule memory to function fairly

## Culture analysis rules

When the user asks why eurogames spread in hobby circles, explain that hobby gamers often act as:

- discoverers
- recommenders
- local teachers
- repeat players who stabilise a game’s reputation

Do not frame the audience as monolithic. Hobby gamers include casual enthusiasts, committed collectors, reviewers, club regulars, and deeply analytical players.

## When this subskill is most useful

- writing product positioning copy
- designing for hobby adoption
- explaining why a game that works in clubs may fail with families
- analysing community discourse and enthusiasm cycles

## Common pitfalls Claude should flag

- Designing only for rules connoisseurs and forgetting ordinary table pleasure
- Assuming hobby gamers want maximum complexity
- Assuming thematic taste is uniform
- Confusing collector appetite with player delight

## Best practice

A good eurogame for hobby gamers should reward repeated play without becoming contemptuous of first-time players.
